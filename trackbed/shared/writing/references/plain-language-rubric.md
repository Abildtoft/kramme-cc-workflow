# Plain-Language Editing Rubric

Use this rubric to improve reader success without flattening technical meaning or authorial tone.

## Priority Order

When editing goals conflict, preserve them in this order:

1. factual meaning, evidence, uncertainty, citations, and protected text
2. the reader's ability to identify the point and required action
3. the source's intended tone and required format
4. structural clarity and ease of scanning
5. concision and stylistic polish

Never trade a higher priority for a lower one.

## Reader Contract

Establish these answers before rewriting:

- Who is expected to read this?
- What should they understand, decide, or do afterward?
- What must they know before they can do that safely?
- Which caveats or uncertainties could change their decision?
- Which terminology, format, or tone does the context require?

Do not invent missing answers. Ask only when the ambiguity would materially change the result.

## Information Order

- Start with the answer, action, decision, or outcome the reader came for.
- Follow with evidence, conditions, and necessary detail.
- Put origin stories, chronology, and secondary background later unless sequence is itself the subject.
- Give each section a distinct reader-facing purpose.
- Keep a qualification close to the claim it limits.
- Remove repeated setup and conclusions that add no information.

The first paragraph should let a scanning reader identify why the document matters. Each later paragraph should make the next useful point.

## Sentences and Paragraphs

- Name the actor when responsibility matters and the source supports it.
- Prefer active constructions when they make the actor and action clearer.
- Give a sentence one dominant job. Split it when independent claims compete for attention.
- Keep tightly related conditions, exceptions, and qualifications together.
- Give a paragraph one topic or step. Start a new paragraph when the reader's question changes.
- Treat length as a diagnostic. Shorten nested or heavily qualified sentences when splitting them preserves the relationship between ideas.
- Use natural rhythm rather than forcing every sentence to the same length.

## Words and Terminology

- Prefer familiar words over formal substitutes when both mean the same thing.
- Prefer concrete verbs over abstract noun phrases when the actor and action are known.
- Replace vague quantities and timelines with supported specifics.
- Remove filler, empty intensifiers, and promotional claims that add no evidence.
- Keep domain terms, product names, and precise technical language when simpler wording would be less accurate.
- Explain an unfamiliar abbreviation or term at first use when an explanation is available from the source.
- Repeat the same term for the same concept. Do not rotate synonyms merely for variety.

No individual word is forbidden. Judge wording by accuracy, audience, and context.

## Headings, Lists, and Links

- Use headings that describe the section's content or outcome, not generic labels such as "Overview" when a more specific heading is available.
- Use sentence case unless a required template or project convention says otherwise.
- Use bullets for parallel facts, options, or requirements.
- Use numbered lists only when order matters.
- Keep each list item focused on one parallel unit and use consistent grammar across the list.
- Use link text that identifies the destination or task. Preserve the original URL exactly.
- Use emphasis sparingly and preserve emphasis required by a template or established convention.
- Prefer prose over a table when the content does not require repeated field-by-field comparison.

## Genre Guidance

### Reports and Decision Notes

- Lead with the conclusion, recommendation, or decision needed.
- Follow with evidence, tradeoffs, risks, and background.
- Distinguish observed facts from interpretation and recommendation.

### Guidance and Procedures

- State the outcome and prerequisites before the steps.
- Use numbered steps for the sequence the reader must follow.
- Put warnings immediately before the action that creates the risk.
- State the expected result when it helps the reader verify success.

### Technical Documentation

- Preserve commands, identifiers, interface names, code, and established terminology.
- Explain why a step or constraint matters when that knowledge prevents mistakes.
- Keep prerequisites, supported environments, failure modes, and verification visible.
- Do not replace precise technical terms with approximate everyday language.

### Research Notes

- Lead with the answer to the research question.
- Keep citations next to the claims they support.
- Separate findings, inference, conflicts, and unknowns.
- Preserve source qualifications and confidence levels.

### Summaries

- State what changed or what the source concludes before recounting detail.
- Preserve the facts that affect decisions, risk, scope, or follow-up work.
- Omit examples only when they are not evidence and their removal does not change meaning.

## Protected Content

Leave these unchanged unless the user explicitly requests a scoped edit and the content can safely change:

- attributed quotations
- citations and reference identifiers
- code blocks, inline code, commands, paths, and URLs
- API, schema, type, class, function, and configuration names
- literal interface labels
- legal, contractual, compliance, and policy wording
- data tables whose exact values or labels are significant
- required headings, placeholders, and checklists in templates

Reflowing surrounding prose must not change what protected content refers to.

## Final Check

Before returning the rewrite, verify:

- Can the intended reader identify the point or next action from the opening?
- Does the information arrive in the order the reader needs it?
- Are actors, actions, quantities, dates, and conditions concrete where the source supports them?
- Did every factual claim, caveat, citation, and uncertainty survive accurately?
- Did any wording become simpler but less precise?
- Did any protected text or link destination change?
- Does the structure help scanning without adding decorative formatting?
- Can any remaining words be removed without losing meaning, tone, or traceability?
